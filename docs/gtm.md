# The Pivot: Go-to-Market Strategy

**Product:** The Pivot. A 14-day relationship crisis reset for when love needs logic.
**Price:** $37 one-time (raising to $57 after persistence upgrade)
**URL:** v0-the-pivot-app.vercel.app
**Goal:** 100 paid users in 90 days. $3,700 revenue. 500+ free audit completions. 300+ email captures.
**Budget:** $0 initial. Organic only.
**Date:** April 2026

## 1. Market Context

The relationship crisis space has a massive gap. When someone's partner goes silent at midnight, their options are: therapy (2-week waitlist, $250/session), breakup journaling apps like Mend (passive, younger demographic), dating apps (assumes they're ready to move on), or scrolling Reddit for contradictory advice from strangers. Nothing addresses the acute phase of a relationship crisis with a structured, time-bound programme.

The demand is undeniable. #breakup has 42B+ views on TikTok. "Should I text my ex" gets searched millions of times per month. "Why did my partner go silent" is a question with no authoritative, structured answer anywhere online. People in crisis are searching, scrolling, and desperate for someone to name what they're feeling and tell them what to do right now.

The Pivot sits in a category of one. It is not a therapy app, not a dating app, not a meditation app. It is a 14-day structured reset that meets someone in crisis and walks them through to clarity. The proprietary language (Shutdown Cycle, The Dimming Effect, The Caring Paradox, Safety Vault) gives people words for experiences that currently have no name. That language is the brand moat. When someone says "I'm in a Shutdown Cycle," they're speaking Pivot.

The broader opportunity is the funnel upstream. The Pivot at $37 is the entry point. 20% of Pivot users converting to Love Mastery IQ memberships ($49/mo or $99/mo) makes the lifetime value of a Pivot customer $150+. That changes the math on every acquisition channel.

**Competitive landscape:**
- Mend: breakup recovery app, passive journaling, skews younger, no structured programme, no crisis-specific tools
- BetterHelp/Talkspace: generic therapy platforms, expensive, long waitlists, not designed for acute crisis
- Self-help books: not interactive, not available at 2am, not personalized
- Free content (YouTube, Reddit, Google): overwhelming, contradictory, no structure
- The Pivot: the only tool built specifically for the first 14 days of relationship crisis, with a diagnostic, daily structure, and crisis-specific tools like the Safety Vault and Panic Button

## 2. Launch Strategy

### Pre-Launch (Weeks -8 to -1)

The app is already built and deployed. "Pre-launch" means building the audience and content engine before announcing availability. The goal is to have 200+ email subscribers and 50+ social followers who are primed for the product before the public launch moment.

**Core activities:**
- Stand up the full content pipeline (Claude > Chatterbox > Remotion) and validate it produces quality output at volume
- Begin posting short-form video content daily across TikTok, Instagram Reels, and YouTube Shorts (Channel C: Love Mastery IQ)
- Extract the System Audit as a standalone page with email capture gating the result
- Build the 3-email GHL nurture sequence
- Write and publish the first 5 AEO blog posts on path2lovemastery.com
- Create the 3 shareable audit profile result cards (Integrated, Compartmentalized, Shutdown Cycle)
- Soft-test messaging. Which hooks get engagement? Which pattern names resonate? Which crisis types generate the most audit completions?

### Soft Launch (Weeks 1-2)

Soft launch means the product is live, payment is working, but you are not making a public announcement. Traffic comes from content and direct outreach. This is the period where you validate the full funnel end-to-end: content > audit > email capture > nurture > purchase.

**Core activities:**
- Drive traffic from social content to the System Audit
- Monitor the entire funnel for drop-off points
- Get 10-20 paid users and collect qualitative feedback
- Fix any friction in the payment flow, email sequence, or onboarding
- Identify which content types and platforms drive the most audit completions
- Ask early users for permission to quote their experience (anonymized or named)

### Public Launch (Week 3)

Public launch is the moment you announce The Pivot as a product. This is when you leverage the content library, early social proof, and accumulated email list for a coordinated push.

**Core activities:**
- Announce across all channels simultaneously: social, email, blog
- Publish a "launch story" piece: the personal story behind building The Pivot, written in Alison's voice
- Offer a limited-time launch incentive (e.g., include a bonus Guided Reflection audio for the first 50 buyers)
- Reach out to coaches, therapists, and personal development creators who might recommend the tool to their clients
- Push the best-performing content from soft launch as the lead content during launch week
- Target: 30-50 paid users by end of launch week

Build the 3-email GHL sequence for audit completers:

By now the Remotion pipeline should be producing 7-10 videos per week efficiently. The weekly rhythm should be locked in:

## 5. Post-Launch Growth (Weeks 1-12)

### Weeks 1-4: Optimize the Funnel

The first month is about finding the numbers. What is the actual conversion rate from audit to email? From email to purchase? From video view to audit? Every number tells you where to focus.

Expected benchmarks based on the funnel structure:
- Video to audit click-through: 1-3% (standard for short-form CTA)
- Audit start to completion: 60-70% (10 questions is fast)
- Audit completion to email capture: 40-50% (gated result creates strong motivation)
- Email to purchase (3-email sequence): 5-8%
- Overall video-to-purchase: roughly 1 sale per 5,000-10,000 video views

If any stage underperforms, that's where you focus. Low audit completion? The questions might be confusing. Low email capture? The result teaser isn't compelling enough. Low email-to-purchase? The nurture sequence needs work.

Continue publishing 7-10 videos per week and 2+ blog posts per week. The content machine does not stop.

### Weeks 5-8: Double Down on What Works

By week 5, you'll know which platforms, content types, and hooks drive the most conversions. The data will tell you whether TikTok or Instagram Reels is the primary channel. It will tell you whether pattern explainers or emotional hooks drive more audit completions. It will tell you whether the "3 Types of Relationship Crisis" framing or the "Shutdown Cycle" framing resonates more.

Double your investment in the top-performing channel and content type. If TikTok is driving 70% of audit completions, shift to 2 videos/day on TikTok and reduce other platforms to maintenance posting.

Begin the programmatic SEO push. Create the WordPress page template and generate the first 20-50 pages targeting long-tail keyword combinations: "breakup recovery for [profession]," "[attachment style] breakup recovery," "no contact rule for [situation]." Each page links to the System Audit. This is a slow-burn channel that compounds over months. The Distribution & Marketing Plan outlines the keyword patterns in detail.

### Weeks 9-12: Scale and Upstream

By week 9, the $37 product should be generating consistent daily sales. The target is 100 paid users by day 90. If the funnel is converting at the expected rates, that requires roughly 2,500 audit completions over 90 days, or about 28 per day. At 40+ videos per month and 20+ blog posts driving search traffic, that's achievable.

This is also when the upstream funnel activates. Pivot users who completed the 14-day programme are the warmest possible leads for Love Mastery IQ membership ($49/mo or $99/mo). Build a post-programme email sequence: "Your 14 days are done. Here's what comes next." Offer a discounted first month or a free trial of the membership. Even a 10% conversion rate from Pivot to LMI means 10 members at $49-99/mo in recurring revenue.

Begin researching niche newsletter acquisitions as outlined in the Distribution & Marketing Plan. A 10K-subscriber breakup/relationship newsletter at $5-15K could be ROI-positive from the first send if the persistence upgrade is shipped and the product is solid. Do not buy until Supabase persistence is live.

## 7. Content Strategy

### Content Pillars

Every piece of content serves one of three purposes:

**Pillar 1: Name the Feeling.** Content that makes someone stop scrolling because it describes exactly what they're experiencing. "They went silent and your brain is telling you it means they don't care." This is the top-of-funnel content. It builds trust and drives audit completions. The 7 video scripts in Batch 1 are all Pillar 1 content.

**Pillar 2: Explain the Pattern.** Content that gives people language for what's happening. The Shutdown Cycle, The Dimming Effect, The Caring Paradox. These are the concepts that make people feel seen and give them something to share. Pattern explainer videos, quote cards, and blog posts fall here.

**Pillar 3: Show the Path.** Content that demonstrates what the 14-day reset looks like. Daily Anchor teasers, Safety Vault demos, audit result reveals. This is the content that converts interest into purchases.

### Monthly Volume Targets

- **Short-form video:** 40+ per month (7-10 per week via the Remotion pipeline)
- **Blog posts:** 5+ per month (AEO-optimized, targeting the 20 questions)
- **Email sends:** 4 per month to the full list (weekly newsletter) plus automated nurture sequences
- **Quote cards/graphics:** 10+ per month for X/Twitter and Pinterest
- **Pillar content:** 1-2 per month (longer-form YouTube video or podcast episode on Channel A for audio distribution)

### The Remotion Pipeline in Practice

The Distribution & Marketing Plan documents this in full, but here is the operational summary. Claude writes 7-10 video scripts per batch, drawing from the 20 hooks list and expanding into new hooks as data shows what works. Alison reviews and approves scripts in a single 30-minute session. Chatterbox generates voiceovers in Alison's cloned voice. Remotion renders branded videos with motion graphics, animated text, and branded colours synced to the voiceover. Total founder time per batch: approximately 1 hour. Total cost: $0 beyond the Claude subscription.

The first 7 scripts are written and ready for production (see `+/Marketing/Pivot Video Scripts - Batch 1.md`). They cover: "They Went Silent" (Shutdown Cycle), "The Text You Want to Send" (Safety Vault), "The Dimming Effect," "Brain on Breakup" (neurochemistry), "3 Types of Relationship Crisis" (audit funnel), "20 Minutes" (urge surfing), and "Chemistry vs. Clarity."

### AEO Content Approach

Each blog post targets one of the 20 questions people Google during breakups. The structure for every post:

1. Open with the emotional moment (name the feeling, not the framework)
2. Answer the question directly in the first 100 words (for AI citation)
3. Explain the pattern using Pivot proprietary language
4. Provide 3-5 actionable steps
5. Include FAQ schema markup with the question and answer
6. End with the System Audit CTA

The goal is not just to rank in Google but to be the source that Perplexity, ChatGPT, and Gemini cite when someone asks these questions. Structured, authoritative, specific content with clear attribution to path2lovemastery.com and The Pivot.

### Content Repurposing Flow

One pillar piece becomes many derivative pieces:

- 1 podcast episode or YouTube video > 5-7 short-form video clips > 3 quote cards > 1 blog post > 1 email newsletter > 5 tweets/X posts
- 1 blog post > 3 short-form videos (one per key point) > 5 quote graphics > 1 Pinterest infographic
- 1 pattern explainer > 1 animated video > 1 quote card > 1 Twitter thread > 1 carousel post

The existing Claude skills (`/weekly-content-batch`, `/content-repurpose`, `/social-content`) handle this repurposing. The bottleneck is never production. It is always Alison's review and approval bandwidth.

## 8. Community Strategy

### Phase 1: Content Community (Months 1-3)

In the early months, "community" means the audience you're building through content. These are people who follow the social accounts, take the audit, and join the email list. The community strategy is simple: respond to every comment, DM everyone who shares their audit result, and treat the comment section as a coaching moment.

When someone comments on a TikTok with "this is exactly what I'm going through," that is not just engagement. That is a potential customer and advocate. Reply with genuine warmth and a nudge toward the audit: "You're not alone in this. Take the free System Audit if you want to understand the pattern. Link in bio."

### Phase 2: User Community (Months 3-6)

Once you have 100+ paid users, create a private space for Pivot alumni. This could be a simple Telegram group, a Circle community, or a dedicated channel in the broader Path2Love Mastery community. The purpose is twofold: retention (keep users connected after their 14 days) and upstream conversion (Pivot alumni are the warmest leads for Love Mastery IQ membership).

Content for the community: weekly Q&A, pattern deep-dives, "where I am now" progress shares. Alison's presence here should be light but consistent. One post per week. The community manages itself if the founding members set the right tone.

### Phase 3: Practitioner Network (Months 6+)

Coaches and therapists who recommend The Pivot to their clients become an informal distribution network. Offer them a practitioner account with access to the full programme, a co-branded referral link, and a simple commission (e.g., 30% of each referred sale). Ten active practitioners each referring 2-3 clients per month is 20-30 sales per month on autopilot.

### Viral Loop: The Audit Share

The System Audit result is the community strategy's secret weapon. When someone shares "I just took a Relationship System Audit. I'm in a Shutdown Cycle," they are doing three things: expressing identity (people share personality-test style content), normalizing relationship crisis (reducing stigma and increasing the addressable audience), and driving qualified traffic back to the audit. Every share is free acquisition.

Make the share experience frictionless. A share button on the result page. Prefilled text. A beautiful branded image. Optimized for Instagram Stories, Twitter, and text messaging. The Distribution & Marketing Plan details the viral artifact strategy.

## 11. Risks

### Risk 1: Content Doesn't Convert

**The scenario:** Videos get views but nobody clicks through to the audit. Or people take the audit but don't enter their email. Or emails go out but nobody buys.

**Mitigation:** The funnel has four stages and each can be optimized independently. If video-to-audit is low, test different hooks and CTAs. If audit-to-email is low, make the gated result more compelling (show a partial result and gate the detailed breakdown). If email-to-purchase is low, rewrite the nurture sequence, add social proof, or test a lower price point temporarily. The key is instrumenting every stage so you see exactly where the drop-off happens.

**Likelihood:** Medium. The hooks are strong and tested in adjacent niches. The audit-as-lead-magnet pattern has high proven conversion rates across similar products (quizzes typically capture emails at 40-60%). The risk is in the purchase step, which is why the nurture sequence matters.

### Risk 2: Chatterbox Voice Quality

**The scenario:** The AI-cloned voice sounds robotic, unnatural, or uncanny-valley enough that it undermines trust. People click away from the video before the hook lands.

**Mitigation:** Test early and be honest about quality. If Chatterbox output isn't warm and natural enough for the crisis-sensitive audience, two fallback options: Alison records voiceovers manually (increases founder time but ensures quality), or use ElevenLabs as a paid backup. The scripts themselves are the valuable asset. The voice delivery method can be swapped.

**Likelihood:** Low to medium. Chatterbox was specifically chosen because Alison's voice clone was already created. But relationship crisis content requires a specific tone (warm, calm, authoritative) that synthetic voices sometimes struggle with. Test with 3-5 people before scaling.

### Risk 3: Data Loss Before Persistence

**The scenario:** A paying user completes 7 days of the programme, switches devices or clears their browser, and loses all their Safety Vault entries, check-in history, and progress. They're furious. They ask for a refund. They leave a negative review.

**Mitigation:** This is the single biggest technical risk. Currently, The Pivot uses localStorage which is device-specific and ephemeral. The Supabase persistence migration must be prioritized. In the interim: set clear expectations in the onboarding ("best experienced on one device, bookmark this page") and keep volume low during soft launch so any data loss issues affect fewer people. Do not buy paid traffic or a newsletter until persistence is shipped.

**Likelihood:** High if left unaddressed. This is a when, not an if, problem. Prioritize the Supabase migration.

### Risk 4: Platform Algorithm Changes

**The scenario:** TikTok deprioritizes the type of content you're posting, Instagram changes Reels distribution, or a platform bans AI-generated voice content.

**Mitigation:** Never depend on a single platform. The strategy deliberately spans five platforms (TikTok, Instagram, YouTube, Pinterest, X) plus owned channels (blog, email). If any one platform underperforms, the content repurposes to others. The AEO blog strategy is the long-term hedge: Google search and AI citations are more durable than any social algorithm.

**Likelihood:** Medium. Platform changes are constant. But the diversity of channels and the investment in owned media (blog, email list) provide resilience.

### Risk 5: Emotional Sensitivity of the Audience

**The scenario:** Someone in acute crisis encounters a Pivot video or ad that feels exploitative, triggering, or tone-deaf. They call it out publicly. The brand takes a reputation hit.

**Mitigation:** This is why voice matters more than volume. Every piece of content must lead with empathy and name the feeling before explaining it. The scripts in Batch 1 do this well. Never use fear-based marketing ("your relationship is doomed"). Never use shame ("you're the reason they left"). Always validate first, then offer the path. Alison must personally review every script before production. This is the one bottleneck that should never be automated away.

**Likelihood:** Low if the voice guidelines are followed. The brand voice is warm, direct, and non-clinical. It does not pathologize. It does not diagnose. It names the experience and offers a structured response.

### Risk 6: Low Organic Reach

**The scenario:** Despite consistent posting, the accounts never gain traction. Videos get 50-200 views. The math doesn't work at that scale.

**Mitigation:** If organic reach stalls after 30 days of consistent posting, try these in order: (1) Increase posting frequency to 2-3x per day. (2) Engage heavily in comments on related creators' posts to drive profile visits. (3) Test duet/stitch content responding to popular breakup videos. (4) Post during peak crisis hours (10pm-2am when the target audience is scrolling). (5) If nothing moves after 60 days, allocate a small ad budget ($5-10/day) to boost the best-performing content.

**Likelihood:** Medium. Organic growth on social platforms is never guaranteed. But the breakup niche has extremely high engagement rates, and the proprietary language (Shutdown Cycle, Dimming Effect) gives the content a distinctive hook that generic relationship advice lacks.

### Risk 7: Founder Bandwidth

**The scenario:** Alison is running the broader Path2Love Mastery ecosystem (Love Mastery IQ, coaching, content creation) and doesn't have enough time to review Pivot scripts, approve content, and manage the launch.

**Mitigation:** The entire pipeline was designed to minimize founder time. Script review: 30 minutes per batch. No recording required (Chatterbox handles voice). No video editing required (Remotion handles rendering). The only non-delegatable task is script approval, because voice and emotional sensitivity matter. Everything else runs on the AI pipeline. If bandwidth is still tight, reduce to 5 videos per week instead of 10 and maintain the blog cadence.

**Likelihood:** Medium. This is a real constraint. The mitigation is built into the system design.
